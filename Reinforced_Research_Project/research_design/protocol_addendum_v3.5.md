# Protocol addendum v3.5 (Stage 10) — a pole-aware pool member; written and committed BEFORE any surrogate was fitted to the new problems
Written 2026-11-17. Motivation: two journal decisions said that the paper has limited new contribution and novelty. The paper's method content is a selection procedure over classical models; the selection component is not novel and the forecast component did not improve (Stage 9). The one place where the classical pool fails badly is on responses with **poles inside the parameter range** (undamped Helmholtz problems: best pool errors 0.6–1.4, "no model is usable"), which Gaussian processes, kernel ridge and ridge polynomials cannot represent. The method contribution tried here is a pool member for that case, `PodRational`: POD with a shared-denominator rational regression of the mode coefficients (denominator by Sanathanan–Koerner iterations with variable projection, numerators by weighted ridge, polynomial degrees and ridge chosen on a 25 % hold-out from a fixed grid; tool version 0.4.0). Whether it is novel is not established: rational and AAA-type surrogates for parametric problems exist (searched only superficially so far), and the claim to test is the narrower one that it **rescues the cases the classical pool cannot handle, is chosen by cross-validation when useful, and does no harm elsewhere**.

**What was looked at before this file was written (design information, all on SEEN data).** A synthetic exact rational response (recovered to 3·10⁻⁵ at 120 runs where a polynomial gives 0.3), and one-seed test errors of the member on six seen problems (`helmholtz_L1/L2`, `lshape_L2`, `heatcomp_L2`, `disc_elast_L2`, `reacdiff_fe_L2`; N = 256 and 1024): large gains on `helmholtz_L1` (0.015 against more than 1.3 at N = 1024), `lshape_L2` (0.049 against 1.6 for the GP members, 0.059 for the polynomial), small or no gains elsewhere, and failure at N = 256 on `helmholtz_L2`. The grid of degrees (numerator 3, 4, 6; denominator 1–4; ridge 10⁻⁶, 10⁻³) was extended once (denominator up to 4) before the new problems existed. Four new frequency-response problems were built and unit-tested against theory (resonance of the unit-square cavity at k = π, cantilever first resonance 3.516, passivity, reciprocity, mesh convergence); **no surrogate of any kind had been fitted to them**; their damping ranges were chosen from solver sweeps so that resonance peaks are sharp (amplitude ratio about 20 to 40 over the range at the lowest damping).

**Frozen.** SurroSelect 0.4.0 (default pool of 8 members: the 7 of earlier versions plus `pod_rational` for up to six inputs), the new problems, the run and analysis scripts and these criteria, at the commit that contains this file (hash in `research_design/freeze_v3.5.txt`, recorded before the first run). The run scripts were smoke-tested on the seen problem `lshape_L2`.

## 10.1 New problems (scikit-fem 12.0.2, `problems_tp4.py`; complex linear systems; output = real and imaginary parts at fixed points)
`acoustic_cavity_L1/L2` (p = 4; damped Helmholtz with impedance walls; L1 unit square, wave number 2.6–3.8, impedance 0.01–0.08, one resonance group; L2 L-shaped cavity, wave number 2.2–4.4, impedance 0.005–0.04; inputs wave number, impedance, source position; m = 128 / 96) and `beam_frf_L1/L2` (p = 4; cantilever with Hermite elements, Rayleigh damping, tip mass, load position; L1 frequency 2–6 with the first resonance, L2 frequency 2–30 with the first two; m = 80). Data protocol as before (pool 4096, test 1000, nested subsets).

## 10.2 Experiments
**F**: the tool 0.4.0 (4-fold CV, `refit_all=True`) on the 4 new problems, N ∈ {64, 256, 1024}, seeds 0–2; the **old-pool selection** is the CV-best among the 7 earlier members of the same run (valid because each member's CV and test errors do not depend on the pool). **S**: tuned MLP (16 trials, as before), N ∈ {256, 1024}, same problems and seeds. **H** (no harm): the same tool run on the 52 earlier problems (44-problem suite and the 8 Stage 7 problems), N = 256, seeds 0–1.

## 10.3 Criteria (cells = problem × N, seed-geometric-mean test errors; F has 12 cells, 8 with N ≥ 256)
* **R1 gain**: the new pool's selected error is at most half the old pool's in at least 6 of 12 cells.
* **R2 no loss on F**: the new pool's selected error is more than 10 % worse than the old pool's in at most 1 of 12 cells.
* **R3 selection quality (new pool)**: median regret (selected / best of the 8 members) ≤ 1.5 and at least 75 % of cells ≤ 2.
* **R4 vs network**: the tuned network is better than the new pool's selected model by more than 25 % in at most 3 of 8 cells (N ≥ 256).
* **R5 usability**: in the 8 cells with N ≥ 256 the new pool's selected error is ≤ 0.25 in at least 6, and in at least 2 more cells than the old pool's.
* **R6 no harm elsewhere (H)**: over the 52 problems (seed-mean at N = 256) the median ratio of new to old selected error is ≤ 1.03, and the ratio exceeds 1.10 in at most 5 problems.
* **R7 (reported, no threshold)**: where the new pool is better by more than 25 % on the 52 earlier problems; how often `pod_rational` is selected; **R8** its share of the tool's cross-validation time.

## 10.4 Predictions stated now
R1 will probably hold (6–8 of 12; the N = 64 cells will not gain because the member needs data); R2 will hold; R3 will hold; R4 is uncertain (a tuned network may also fit sharp peaks at N = 1024, less likely at 256); R5 uncertain, most likely met for the beam and the square cavity and not for the L-shaped cavity; R6 will hold because cross-validation protects, but may fail on a few problems where the hold-out inside the member is noisy; R7: gains on `lshape_L2`, `helmholtz_L1` and a few high-contrast problems.

## 10.5 Rules
No criterion, grid, pool member or data protocol changes after the first result; deviations are logged (V19 onwards); analyses added after the results are labelled post hoc. If R1 or R6 fails the member is reported as a negative result and not shipped in the default pool. A positive result is a result about these four frequency-response problems and the 52 earlier ones, not about all parametric problems; the novelty of the member relative to the rational-approximation literature must be judged on a proper literature review, which is part of the paper work after this stage.
