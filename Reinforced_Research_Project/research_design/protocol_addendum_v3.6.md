# Protocol addendum v3.6 (Stage 11) — pole deflation; written and committed BEFORE any surrogate was fitted to the fresh problems
Written 2026-11-17, after Stage 10 (rational pool member: R1 gain, R4 network and R5 usability not met; no harm on 52 problems; helped on single-resonance problems with enough runs, and a tuned network was the only usable model on the cantilever). The user asked for another attempt with a more flexible resonance model that still lets a network take part. **Pole deflation** (class `PoleDeflated`, tool version 0.5.0): if the response is c(μ) = P(μ)/Q(μ) with a shared denominator Q, then Q(μ)·c(μ) is smooth, so any regressor can fit it. Q is estimated by the Sanathanan–Koerner fit of Stage 10 (low-degree polynomial denominator), the deflated field Q(μ)(Y − mean) is regressed by an inner model (a POD + RBF Gaussian process for the pool member; the tuned network for the network arm), and predictions are divided by Q. A 25 % hold-out decides whether to deflate or to use the plain inner model, so the member is never knowingly worse than its inner model on the hold-out. This is a combination of known pieces (rational denominators, variable transformation, Gaussian processes and networks); the claim to test is that it makes the resonance cases usable that neither the classical pool nor the plain network handle.

**What was looked at before this file was written (design information, all on SEEN data).** A synthetic exact rational response (deflated GP error at least five times lower than the plain GP; fall-back to the plain model on smooth data) and one-seed test errors on six seen problems: at N = 1024 the deflated GP gave 0.115 on `beam_frf_L1` (plain GP 1.95, rational member 0.22, best classical 0.80 in Stage 10), 0.69 on `beam_frf_L2` (plain 4.2) and 0.0010 on `acoustic_cavity_L1` (plain 0.025); at N = 256 the hold-out chose the plain model in the three cases looked at (deflation needs data to estimate Q). Two fresh problem families were then built and unit-tested against theory, with **no surrogate of any kind fitted to them**: `mdk_chain` (20 masses and springs, fixed–free, Rayleigh damping; the resonances match the closed-form frequencies 0.0767, 0.2294, 0.3807, 0.530) and `disc_acoustic` (circular cavity with impedance wall; resonances at the zeros of J₁′, J₂′, J₀′: 1.842, 3.055, 3.835); passivity, reciprocity and mesh convergence are tested.

**Frozen.** SurroSelect 0.5.0 (`PoleDeflated`; selection logic and the 7-member default pool unchanged), the fresh problems, the run and analysis scripts and these criteria, at the commit that contains this file (hash in `research_design/freeze_v3.6.txt` before the first run). The run script was smoke-tested on the seen problem `lshape_L1` with a tiny network budget.

## 11.2 Experiments
**F**: tool (7-member default pool + `PoleDeflated(PodGP rbf)`; 4-fold CV; `refit_all`) on the 4 fresh problems, N ∈ {64, 256, 1024}, seeds 0–2; the old-pool selection is the CV-best of the 7 original members in the same run. **S**: plain tuned MLP (16 trials), N ∈ {256, 1024}. **D**: pole-deflated tuned MLP (`PoleDeflated(TunedMLP, 16 trials)`), N ∈ {256, 1024}. **H** (no harm): the tool of F on the 52 earlier problems, N = 256, seeds 0–1.

## 11.3 Criteria (cells = problem × N, seed-geometric means). N = 64 is excluded from the gain and usability criteria: Stage 10 showed no gain at 64 runs and a denominator cannot be identified from so few runs; "N ≥ 256" cells are 8.
* **D1 gain**: the new pool's selected error is at most half the old pool's in at least 4 of the 8 cells with N ≥ 256.
* **D2 no loss**: in at most 1 of all 12 cells the new pool's selected error is more than 10 % worse than the old pool's.
* **D3 selection quality (12 cells)**: median regret ≤ 1.5 and at least 75 % of cells ≤ 2.
* **D4 usability**: in the 8 cells with N ≥ 256 the new pool's selected error is ≤ 0.25 in at least 6, and in at least 2 more cells than the old pool's.
* **D5 deflated network**: the pole-deflated network is better than the plain tuned network by more than 25 % in at least 3 of the 8 cells and worse by more than 25 % in at most 2.
* **D6 best available**: the best of (new pool, plain network, deflated network) is ≤ 0.25 in at least 6 of the 8 cells.
* **D7 vs plain network**: the plain network is better than the new pool's selected model by more than 25 % in at most 3 of the 8 cells.
* **D8 no harm**: over the 52 earlier problems (seed-mean, N = 256) the median ratio of new to old selected error is ≤ 1.03, and the ratio exceeds 1.10 in at most 5.
* **D9 (reported)**: how often the deflated member is selected, its cost.

## 11.4 Predictions stated now
D1 will probably hold (gains at N = 1024 on at least the single-resonance problems, and possibly at 256); D2 will hold (the hold-out fall-back); D3 will hold; D4 is uncertain (about even odds; the multi-resonance level-2 problems are the likeliest failures); D5 is uncertain (deflation may help the network at 1024 but the network has to be retrained three times inside the wrapper); D6 will probably hold; D7 is uncertain; D8 will hold.

## 11.5 Rules
No criterion, grid, pool member or data protocol changes after the first result; deviations are logged (V21 onwards); analyses added afterwards are labelled post hoc. If D1 or D8 fails, the member is reported as a negative result and not added to the default pool. A positive result concerns these four fresh problems and the 52 earlier ones and is the third method attempt in this project: any claim of novelty must be judged against the rational-approximation, variable-transformation and operator-learning literature, which has been searched only in part.
