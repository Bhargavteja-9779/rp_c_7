# Protocol addendum v3.4 (Stage 9) — calibrated budget forecasts; written and committed BEFORE any forecaster was fitted
Written 2026-11-16. Motivation: the second journal's decision said the paper has "limited new contribution and novelty" (the first said "not strong enough"). Our own evidence says the same about the selection component (classical pool, a fixed Gaussian process does almost as well). The component that failed in every evaluation is the **error forecast** of the tool: within a factor 2 in 68 % of 96 cases over sixteen problems, mostly optimistic (Stages 6 and 7). Turning the failing component into a **validated, calibrated budget forecaster** is a method contribution that the data can confirm or refute. Prior work found by a limited search (Mohr & van Rijn 2024; Rosenfeld et al. 2020; LC-PFN, Adriaensen et al. 2023; sample-size determination via learning-type curves; conformal prediction for PDE surrogates) extrapolates learning curves or calibrates the surrogate's prediction; we found no work that calibrates the *budget forecast* of a surrogate for parametric simulations across problems. This is a statement about our search, not a proof of novelty.

**What was looked at before this file was written.** The stored forecasts of Stages 6, 7 and 8 and their failure pattern (optimistic bias, non-monotone curves), and the timings of the model fits (seconds, so a large learning-curve dataset is affordable). No new learning-curve data had been generated and no calibrated forecaster had been fitted. The held-out problems below were seen in Stage 7/8 (their old forecasts failed), so the held-out evaluation is **design-informed in the direction of the bias**, but every parameter of the forecaster is learned from the development problems only.

## 9.1 Data (experiment L; `exp_s9.py`)
Development set: the 44 problems of the benchmark (23 families). Held-out set: the 8 Stage 7 problems (4 families never used by the forecaster), `bracket3d_L1` and `lshape_fine_L1` (pools of 512 runs). For each problem and seed 0–2 (nested training subsets `default_rng(1000+seed).permutation(pool)[:N]`) and each N0 ∈ {64, 128, 256} (N0 ∈ {64, 128} for the two 512-run pools): the tool (SurroSelect 0.2.1, default pool, 4-fold CV, no `refit_all`) selects a model and returns its cross-validated learning curve (four points); the selected member is then refit on the nested subsets of size N0, 2·N0 and 4·N0 and scored on the test inputs. The target of a forecast made at N0 for horizon h ∈ {2, 4} is y = log10 of the test error of the same member trained on h·N0 runs. Cases: 44·3·3·2 = 792 development cases, 168 held-out cases.

## 9.2 Forecasters (all fixed here; log10 scale; cases are (problem, seed, N0, h))
Input of every forecaster: the four points (n_i, e_i) of the cross-validated learning curve, N0, h, the number of inputs p, the number of outputs m and the POD-floor error of the tool. Notation: f0 = the tool's power-law forecast (least squares on the last three points of log e against log n, extrapolated to N = h·N0); r = y − log10 f0 (residual).
* **F0** the tool's forecast (baseline). **F5** carry-forward: y = log10 e₄ (the last point). **F2** f0 plus the training mean of r (per h).
* **F1** shrunk slope: the slope b of F0 replaced by (1−λ)b + λ·b̄ with b̄ the training mean slope per N0 and λ chosen from {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1} by leave-one-family-out within the training data.
* **F3 (proposed)**: ŷ = log10 f0 + g(z), g a ridge regression (penalty 1, standardised features) of r on z = (log2 h, log10 N0, b₃, b₄, curvature, non-monotone flag, log10 e₄, p, log10 m, clipped log10 (best CV error / POD floor)), where b₃ is the slope of the last three points, b₄ of all four, curvature the difference of the slopes of the last two and the first two points, and the non-monotone flag is 1 if any e_{i+1} > e_i. **Interval**: ŷ ± q, q the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n quantile of the absolute residuals |r − g(z)| obtained by leave-one-family-out cross-fitting inside the training data (split-conformal style), α = 0.2 (80 % interval) and α = 0.1 (90 %).
* **F4** like F3 with the interval scaled by a second ridge regression of log |r − g(z)| on z (normalised conformal).
Development: leave-one-family-out (LOFO) over the 23 families: for each held-out family all forecasters are fitted on the other 22 families and applied to the cases of the held-out family. Final: all forecasters fitted on all development cases (frozen, parameters saved in `results/s9/calibration_v1.json`) and applied once to the held-out set. Inference unit: the problem (cluster bootstrap over problems, 2000 resamples; seeds and N0 within a problem are not independent).

## 9.3 Criteria (held-out set, frozen F3; "share" = fraction of cases with |y − ŷ| ≤ log10 2)
* **K1 coverage**: the empirical coverage of the nominal 80 % interval is between 0.70 and 0.90.
* **K2 accuracy**: the median |y − ŷ| of F3 is at most 0.75 times that of F0, and the share within a factor 2 of F3 exceeds that of F0 by at least 10 percentage points.
* **K3 features help**: the share of F3 is not lower than that of F2 by more than 2 points **and** the median 80 % interval width of F3 or F4 is at most 0.9 times that of F2 with a constant-width interval at equal nominal level (otherwise we report that a constant correction does as well).
* **K4 sharpness**: the median width of the 80 % interval (in decades of log10 error) is at most 1.0.
* **K5 decision value**: among cases in which the upper 80 % bound of F3 is below a target (1 % or 0.1 %), the target is actually reached in at least 80 % of them (evaluable if at least 10 such cases for the target; reported per target).
The same quantities are reported for the LOFO development evaluation (descriptive).

## 9.4 Predictions stated now
K2 will hold (removing the optimistic bias is easy). K1 is the main risk: conformal intervals calibrated across families are valid under exchangeability that held-out families may violate; I predict coverage between 0.65 and 0.85, so about even odds that K1 fails on the lower side. K3 will probably fail on the width part (a constant correction and constant interval will be about as good as the feature model); if so, the honest summary is "bias correction plus conformal width", not a learned feature model. K4 will probably fail at N0 = 64 and may fail overall. K5 will probably hold for the 1 % target and may not be evaluable for 0.1 %.

## 9.5 Rules
Nothing in §9.2–9.3 changes after the first forecaster is fitted. The development evaluation may be used to choose λ for F1 and nothing else. Any additional analysis after seeing held-out results is labelled post hoc. A failed fit counts as infinite error; such cases are excluded from the forecast data and counted. The calibrated forecaster is shipped in the tool only if K1 and K2 hold on the held-out set; otherwise the tool keeps its current forecast with a documented caveat and the paper reports the negative result.
