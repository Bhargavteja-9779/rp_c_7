## 3. Findings

### CRITICAL

**C1. The headline causal claim ("advantage explained by marginal-likelihood fitting", abstract, 5.3, 6(ii), 8) is not isolated by the controls; the KRR comparator is structurally handicapped.** Evidence: (a) `methods.py::PodKRR` uses ridge `lam*N` with `lam` log-uniform in [1e-10, 1e-2] and `ell0` in [0.1, 3]x sqrt(p), 8 random draws, one shared length-scale; the GP's white-noise floor is 1e-10 on normalised data and its signal variance can reach 1e3, so the KRR cannot approach the near-interpolation regime that errors of 1e-6 need (effective ridge floor 3e-9 to 1e-7 versus about 1e-13). (b) I re-ran KRR on bratu_L1 (seed 0 subset, same code, same 80/20 validation split, selection by validation error only, no marginal likelihood, r = 16, 2 kernels, small grid) with ridge allowed down to 1e-14: test error 7.0e-07 at N=256 (Exp D registered KRR, 3-seed mean 7.0e-04; POD+GP 1.4e-06) and 2.4e-07 at N=1024 (registered KRR 1.9e-04; POD+GP 3.9e-07; best NN 8.8e-04). Restricting the same grid to the registered ranges gives 2.7e-05 and 1.5e-05 (still 20-40x worse than GP). On heatp_L1 (N=256) the wide-range KRR is 2.6e-05 versus POD+GP 8e-06 (3x). So in the smoothest cells that drive the "14x, up to 490x" statistic, a validation-tuned KRR matches POD+GP once its ridge range is not truncated; the gap measures search-space truncation and 8-draw coarseness, not the absence of marginal likelihood. (c) D2 was pre-registered as the complement of D1 ("otherwise"), and M-MLII compares GP-iso (continuous ML-II over length-scale, noise, amplitude) with KRR (8 random draws), so both rules confound "ML-II" with "fine continuous optimisation over a wider hyper-parameter range". (d) POD+Poly, which has no kernel and no ML-II but the same random search, is also a median 9.9x worse than POD+GP in the same 52 cells, and still beats the best NN by > 0.1 decade in 23 of those 52 cells (4 instances at N=1024, 2 by > 1 decade): simple, well-regularised smooth regressors beat these networks without ML-II. Impact: the sentence "the advantage is attributable to marginal-likelihood fitting rather than to the linear subspace" in the abstract and 8 is not supported; what is supported is "GP regression with continuously optimised hyper-parameters (and an unrestricted effective ridge) beats 8-draw-tuned KRR/Poly and under-trained MLPs on smooth low-p maps, and POD is not needed". Fix: either (i) re-run Exp D with a KRR whose ridge range spans the GP's effective range (lam down to 1e-14, length-scale to 100) and with validation-based continuous optimisation, and a GP with hyper-parameters chosen by 8 random draws (no ML-II), then re-state D/E; or (ii) change the claim everywhere to "hyper-parameter optimisation quality (the GP's continuous ML-II fit versus 8 random draws) rather than the subspace", state that marginal likelihood per se was not isolated, and drop "explained"/"attributable".

### MAJOR

**M1. Exp G reported only on the subset where it supports the story; the full table contradicts "NNs are competitive or better on moving/discontinuous features" (6(iii), 8).** From `expG_cells.csv` (GP on the full field, no POD, B = 2) versus the best NN: at N=1024 the no-POD GP is clearly better in 18 of 24 instances (POD+GP: 12), NN-clear falls from 9 to 5, and heat2d_L2 (ȳ -0.33 -> +0.69), incl_L1 (-0.25 -> +0.75), incl_L2 (-0.13 -> +0.48), advdiff_L2, wave_L2, burgers_L2 flip toward the GP. At N=256 NN-clear 8 -> 6, at N=64 13 -> 7. So the NL wins in the "LS vs NL" map are partly an artefact of POD truncation (r <= 32; kernel hyper-parameters fitted on 4 modes) rather than evidence that neural output maps handle these features better. G1 ("subspace truncation is not what makes the family accurate") is true in the GP-clear cells only. Fix: add a table of gp_full versus best NN for all 72 cells (and 24 instances at each N); rewrite 6(iii) as "NNs beat the POD+GP pipeline on ...; a no-POD GP beat them on several of these"; temper the 5.1 sentence on moving/discontinuous features.

**M2. Magnitudes and the N=1024 headline counts are tied to an under-trained NN and to a step-count discontinuity; "ROBUST" (Exp F) overstates what was tested.** (a) At N=1024 the best NN error is 5e-4 to 1.1e-3 in 11 of the 12 instances with ȳ > 2 (3.5e-3 for burgers_L1; direct, bottleneck and POD+MLP individually 4e-4 to 1.7e-3 in those 11), while POD+GP spans 4e-7 to 1.6e-5 over all 12: an NN floor that does not move with the problem points to a training limit, not to the approximability of the maps. (b) The median NL log-error gain per doubling of N is 0.10 (256->512) and 0.24 (512->1024) (LS: 0.23 and 0.22): the NN gets twice the Adam steps at N=1024, which inflates NL at exactly the N used for the headline counts 13/3/8 and Table 1. The manuscript confines this caveat to the crossover (H1b) discussion in 5.1; the 13/3/8 and the 36-instance Table 1 are all at N=1024 and are presented without it. (c) Exp F's "L-BFGS" arm is capped at 400 iterations and changed the training loss by < 1% in 21% of the 180 refits (33% for the bottleneck, 20% for POD+MLP); the NN floor only drops from 6.8e-04 to 2.0e-04, still about 2 to 3 decades above POD+GP in the decisive instances, so F shows that the tested refits do not close the gap, not that the gap is optimiser-independent. In the three NL-clear F instances the NL margin grows (fisher_L2 -0.47 -> -0.56, heat2d_L2 -0.34 -> -0.59), contradicting the 5.1 statement "the margin is at most 0.44 decade" once training is stronger. (d) Abstract "advantage reached up to three decades" is for the default budget; after Exp F it is 2.7 (bratu_L1). Fix: add the step-matched (full-batch or 2x-epoch) N=1024 counts or flag N=1024 as step-confounded in Table 1/2; rename the F verdict "not overturned by the tested refits (<= 400 L-BFGS iterations)"; qualify the abstract.

**M3. Design properties that affect inference are in the repo but not in the manuscript.** (a) Search draws are shared across all instances and all N within a (seed, method) (deviations_v2 V5): the "B = 8 random search" is 3 distinct draws per method for the whole study, so the 3 seeds are not independent replicates of the tuning, the "seed sign-consistency" (95.5%, 87%) and the "config-draw halves" (83%) measure much less than they appear to, and nothing in 5-7 tells the reader. (b) LS selects among 3 methods and NL among 2 (pre-commitment (e) of addendum v2.2 says this would be reported); not discussed. (c) The "73% of selected NN configs at an edge" statistic (5.4, 5.5, 7) is presented as evidence of a restrictive search space, but `audit/search_space_boundary.py` itself records that about 0.8 is expected under uniform sampling; my recomputation with the same definition gives chance 0.85 versus observed 0.73. The statistic carries no evidence of edge pile-up (only width 256/depth 4 are mildly over-selected: 67% versus 56% chance). Fix: add a limitation sentence for (a) and (b); delete or correct (c).

**M4. A2 "replicated on 7 of 7" and "exact p = 1.7e-06" over-read a near-tautological test.** The 7 qualifiers are four authors-designed smooth families (reacdiff, helm, heatp, diff1d), six with p = 3, chosen after the authors had seen that smooth p = 3 problems were decisive LS wins (addendum v2.1 motivation); the binomial treats the 7 as independent although they are 2 levels of each of 4 families, uses a baseline rate (3/20) from a different instance population, and the pre-committed family-level report ("number of families in which all qualifying instances are decisive": 4 of 4, family-level p0^4 = 5e-4) is not given. The control (diff1d_L2, f1 = -1.72, p = 8) has ȳ = 0.9967, essentially the same as the "decisive" ones, so f1 < -2 is not shown to separate anything (the manuscript says "also had 1.00"). Abstract "held in 7 of 7 pre-selected new instances" is arithmetically right but is evidence of "smooth low-p problems are won by GP", not of a validated pre-selection rule. Fix: report 7/7 (4/4 families) descriptively, drop the p-value or give the family-level one, and call the control "0.997".

**M5. Mechanism cell selection and mixed outcome definitions.** Exp D/E/G select "GP-clear cells" with an oracle comparator (best NL by test error of the seed-mean, a different quantity from the validation-selected family ȳ used in Tables 1-2 and Exp A), pool (instance, N) cells that are not independent (52 cells from 14 instances; 28 from 13, with 6 instances contributing 3 each), and Exp B's "15 of 48 class changes" uses oracle-by-test classes (max difference to the validation classes 0.24 decade) with LS choosing the best of 3 and NL of 2 by test error. Conditioning on "POD+GP-ARD clearly beats NN" also selects cells where ARD happens to be favourable, which biases iso/ARD and KRR/ARD ratios upward (regression to the mean); with iso/ARD this matters because the median is only 1.9. Exp E: iso/ARD is within 3x in only 71% of cells (q75 = 3.46, 8 of 28 cells > 3x), so "rather than anisotropy" is true for the median only; the pre-registered rule is median-only. Fix: report the classes under both definitions, per-instance (not per-cell) summaries, and the share > 3x for iso/ARD in 5.3/Table 4.

## 4. Verdict

**Major revision.** The arithmetic is trustworthy: all 27 numbers I recomputed match (counts, tests, Exp A-G statistics, robustness), the pre-registered verdicts follow the written criteria, the chronology of preregistration commits is consistent, and the negative confirmatory results are reported honestly. The problem is inference, not bookkeeping. The abstract's central mechanistic sentence ("explained by marginal-likelihood fitting ... not the subspace") rests on a kernel-ridge control whose ridge/length-scale range and 8-draw search cannot reach the near-interpolating regime where the GP wins by orders of magnitude (a validation-tuned KRR with a wider ridge range matched POD+GP in my probe); the no-POD GP result is shown only on the subset that supports the narrative while the full table shows it beating the networks where POD+GP does not; the large LS-over-NN gaps and the N=1024 headline counts are entangled with an under-trained, step-count-discontinuous network whose floor (about 5e-4 to 1e-3) is independent of the problem; and several design properties known to the authors (shared search draws, 3-versus-2 members, an uninformative edge statistic) are absent from the text. None of this requires new science beyond one rerun of Exp D/E with an unrestricted KRR/GP-random-search control and one table for Exp G; if those are done (or the claims are reworded to "hyper-parameter optimisation quality of GP regression, not subspace; marginal likelihood per se not isolated"), the paper is a defensible benchmark note with honestly negative confirmatory findings.
