## 3. Are the "reading depth" disclosures honest?

Statement in v3 §2: "of the cited works, only Westermann et al. (about 95% of the text) and Zhang et al. (about 93%) were read beyond abstracts; the absence of a pilot predictor in other sources rests on abstracts and searches (arXiv, Crossref, web), not on full-text review, and patents were not searched."

Assessment: **broadly honest in direction, but imprecise, internally inconsistent and slightly more favourable than the evidence.**

1. Inconsistent with v1. v1 (`manuscript/Final_Manuscript.md`, §2 Positioning) says "two preprints were read in part (about 100,000 characters each)". The literature matrix says Westermann "~200k of 212k characters" and Zhang "~100k of 108k". Both cannot hold for Westermann (100k vs 200k). My own plain-text extraction of the arXiv HTML gives about 152k characters for Westermann and about 82k for Zhang, so neither "212k" nor "108k" is reproducible with a simple HTML-to-text conversion; they probably came from a different extraction (e.g. PDF with LaTeX). The "95%/93%" figures are false precision: 200/212 = 94.3% and 100/108 = 92.6%, and "about 95%" rounds up. A reader cannot check them.
2. "Only Westermann and Zhang were read beyond abstracts" implies that every other cited item was at least read at abstract level. The repository's own records show otherwise: Guo & Hesthaven "title-level; paper not read" (`references_verified.csv`); Hesthaven & Ubbiali, Lu 2021, Li 2021, Kovachki 2023, Cohen & DeVore, Rasmussen & Williams were verified by Crossref metadata only (Crossref carries no abstract for several), and `arxiv_abstracts_core.json` / `arxiv_new_candidates_abstracts.json` contain no stored abstract for Batlle (matrix says "abstract only"; no artefact supports it). Disclosure should say "metadata, titles or abstracts only".
3. The disclosure restricts the evidential weight of the negative claim ("no pilot predictor elsewhere") to "abstracts and searches". That is appropriate, but the search itself had a zero-hit key query (see §2), a coverage gap that is not disclosed. v3 also hides that the search found only 12 + 10 abstract records in total.
4. Positive: the claims actually attributed to Westermann and Zhang match what I could check (Westermann full text contains no GP/kernel baseline and no pilot-type predictor; it does say hyper-parameter tuning "is crucial to achieving good results" in its conclusions, which is relevant to claim 4 and is not cited). The disclosure that patents were not searched is honest, and v3 labels the preregistrations as author-held git histories rather than independent registry entries.
5. One mis-statement of source content (Huang & Stuart "end-to-end cost"; row 20) shows that an abstract-only citation was characterised beyond what the abstract says, in a paper whose disclosure says abstracts were read. This is a content error, not necessarily a dishonest reading-depth claim, but it is the sort of error the disclosure is meant to flag.

Recommended wording: replace the percentages by "full text of the arXiv HTML versions was read for Westermann et al. and Zhang et al.; for all other cited works only titles, abstracts or Crossref/arXiv metadata were consulted (title only for Guo & Hesthaven)", and add the search-coverage caveat.

## 4. Final judgement on novelty relative to closest competitors

Closest competitors: Batlle et al. 2024 and Mora et al. 2025 (kernel/GP competitive with neural operators), Bērziņš et al. 2020 (POD+GPR vs ANN), Kontolati et al. 2023 and Westermann et al. 2026 (no universal winner, smoothness-dependent regimes; tuning "crucial"), Lanthaler 2023 (spectral decay of PCA as a learnability obstacle), McGreivy & Hakim 2024 and Dodge et al. 2019 (weak baselines and budget-dependent rankings).

**Classification: DIFFERENTIATED BUT INCOMPLETELY VALIDATED.**

Justification:
- What is already established (so must not be claimed as new): kernel/GP/POD-regression methods are competitive with or better than neural surrogates on many benchmarks; the winning family depends on solution smoothness / n-width decay; neural baselines are often weakly tuned and rankings change with tuning budget. v3's positioning sentence is honest about this.
- What appears differentiated, with no duplicate found: (a) the tie-aware, effect-size map across 32 instances and six budgets with equivalence bands; (b) the mechanism ablations isolating subspace, kernel prior, anisotropy and marginal-likelihood fitting; (c) an out-of-sample, preregistered test of a pilot predictor, reported as **not confirmed**; (d) a quantified tuning-protocol sensitivity. Item (c) is a negative result, so the paper's predictive contribution is null and its value is methodological honesty plus (a), (b), (d).
- Why "incompletely validated": (i) the absence claims are search-limited and the key POD+GP-vs-NN arXiv query returned zero hits, so at least Bērziņš 2020, Mora 2025, Kontolati 2023, Dama & Nair 2026 and Lanthaler 2023 were missed; (ii) the pilot diagnostic has a theoretical antecedent (PCA/singular-value decay, Lanthaler 2023) and a conceptual one (algorithm selection), neither cited; (iii) tuning-budget dependence of rankings is a known ML phenomenon (Dodge, Bouthillier, Grinsztajn, Melis), so contribution (4) is incremental unless framed as the PDE-surrogate quantification; (iv) a budget of 8 random trials per method, synthetic-only instances, and a single repository's git-history preregistration limit external validity; (v) citation defects (Huang & Stuart mischaracterised, Lee & Carlberg misplaced, Greif & Urban and Cohen & DeVore overgeneralised, Bergstra used beyond its scope, missing authors) need correcting.
- Not "ALREADY ESTABLISHED": no found source replicates the combination (a)-(d). Not "WELL DIFFERENTIATED": the headline qualitative result (POD+GP strong, regime-dependent winners) was previously reported in several forms, and the one novel predictive element failed.

## 5. Five-line summary

1. All 22 distinct cited sources in static_v3 exist with correct authors, years and venues; 16 are SUPPORTED, 5 PARTIAL (Lee & Carlberg, Cohen & DeVore, Greif & Urban, Westermann "FNO faster", Bergstra & Bengio; plus one uncited "established" sentence), and 1 UNSUPPORTED: Huang & Stuart are post-training/amortised, not "end-to-end cost" (v1 had this right).
2. Reference-list fixes: Westermann (4th author Zech) and Zhang (4th author Khodak) are incomplete, Williams & Cremaschi pages are 353-358, and Duraisamy should be labelled preprint.
3. Missing work that matters: Bērziņš 2020 (arXiv:2006.13706, POD+GPR vs ANN), Mora 2025 (CMAME 434:117581), Kontolati 2023 (JCP 479:112008), Dama & Nair 2026 (arXiv:2603.00290), Lanthaler 2023 (JMLR 24(318), arXiv:2303.16317, spectral-decay rationale for the diagnostic), and Dodge 2019 / Bouthillier 2021 (budget-dependent rankings).
4. Reading-depth disclosure is directionally honest but the 95%/93% figures conflict with v1's "~100,000 characters each", are not reproducible, and understate that several items were title-only or metadata-only; the key POD+GP-vs-NN arXiv query returned zero hits.
5. Novelty: DIFFERENTIATED BUT INCOMPLETELY VALIDATED; the quantitative, mechanism and tuning-sensitivity contributions look new but the qualitative result is established, the pilot diagnostic is a negative result, and absence claims are search-limited.
